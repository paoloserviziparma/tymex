"I'll do it on Friday": why filling in timesheets from memory costs you hours

17 November 2026 · 3 min read · Tymex

In short

  • A timesheet filled in from memory is inaccurate: memory reconstructs, it doesn't record.
  • The problem isn't laziness, it's effort: when logging is easy, people do it straight away.
  • Five habits are enough for reliable data without wasting time.

Friday, 5:40 pm

You know the scene. It's Friday afternoon, the week is over, and the message arrives: "Don't forget your timesheet."

You open the sheet. Monday… what did you do on Monday morning? Tuesday you were at a customer's, but for how many hours? Wednesday, a long call, then that meeting that was meant to last half an hour.

In the end you write round numbers. Eight hours here, four and four there. It all looks fine. It isn't.

Memory isn't an archive

Memory doesn't work like a recording you can rewind: every time we remember, we reconstruct. And in reconstructing we simplify, round off and forget the small things.

The small things are exactly what slips away: ten minutes on the phone with a customer, a quick fix, half an hour spent looking for a document. One by one they seem like nothing. Added up on a job, they're the difference between making money and breaking even.

It's not laziness, it's effort

People who put off their timesheet aren't lazy. They're doing what anyone does with a boring, awkward task: pushing it forward.

So the answer isn't to nag harder. It's to make logging so easy there's no reason to put it off.

5 habits for a timesheet that works

1. Log at the end of each task

Two minutes as soon as a job or a visit is done. The memory is fresh, the hours are exact, and it's off your mind.

2. Use your phone, don't wait for the PC

If you have to be at your desk to log time, you'll do it late. With an app on your phone you do it wherever you are, even at a customer's or on site.

3. Duplicate recurring tasks

Many days look alike. Copying yesterday's entry and changing only the date is much faster than writing it again.

4. Check the calendar before you finish for the day

A quick look to see whether the day is complete. If something's missing, you notice now, not on Friday.

5. Write a short note

Three words are enough: "valve replaced", "drawing 3 revised". Weeks later, that note will tell you what you actually did.

What's in it for you

Logging every day isn't just for the company. It takes the Friday chore off your plate, gives you hard data when a job takes longer than planned and saves you from rebuilding whole weeks when someone asks "how long did that take?".

In short

Timesheets filled in from memory aren't a discipline problem, they're a method problem. Log straight away, from your phone, duplicating what repeats and checking the calendar before you finish. Five habits, a few seconds a day, and Friday goes back to being just Friday.
