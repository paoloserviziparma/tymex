The perfect service report: what it should include (and what it shouldn't)

24 November 2026 · 3 min read · Tymex

In short

  • A good service report fits in seven fields: the rest is paperwork.
  • It must be quick for the technician to fill in and clear for the office to read.
  • Going digital ends retyping, lost sheets and illegible handwriting.

What a service report is really for

A service report is the document that records what was done, for whom, when and how long it took. The office uses it to invoice, the manager to control jobs and the customer to know what they're paying for.

If it's incomplete or unclear, someone will have to phone the technician to ask. And nobody pays for the time that call takes.

The 7 essential fields

1. Customer

Who the work was done for. It's the first thing the accounts team needs.

2. Job

Every visit should be linked to a job: it's the only way to know what it costs and what it earns.

3. Activity or service

The type of work done: maintenance, installation, support, design. Choosing from a set list is better than free text, so the data can be compared.

4. Date

The day of the visit. It sounds obvious, but on paper reports it's one of the fields most often missed or wrong.

5. Hours worked

The time actually spent. Without it there's no cost control.

6. Materials and expenses

Spare parts, consumables, travel, tolls. If they're not recorded straight away, they often never get charged.

7. Notes

A few lines on what was done and any problems found. Short, but precise.

What you don't need

A report with too many fields gets filled in badly. Avoid asking for information the office already has, such as the customer's address and tax details, or optional fields nobody reads. Every extra field is another reason to put it off.

The most common mistakes

  • reports filled in days after the visit
  • hours rounded to half or whole days
  • vague descriptions such as "technical visit"
  • forgotten materials and expenses
  • paper sheets lost, soaked or illegible
  • the same visit logged twice

From paper to digital

Going digital solves most of these problems: no sheets to retype in the office, required fields that can't be skipped, pick lists instead of free text and data immediately available to whoever invoices or controls jobs.

The key is that the tool must be faster than paper. If the app takes the technician longer than a pen, they'll go back to the pen.

How Tymex helps

In short

The perfect service report is one the technician is happy to fill in and the office doesn't have to correct. Seven clear fields, filled in straight away, ideally on a phone: everything else is paperwork.
